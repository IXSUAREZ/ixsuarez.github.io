About Simply Endorsed CFI and endorsement guides

Every FAA AC 61-65K endorsement at a glance

Simply Endorsed CFI is a free, searchable reference for all 96 endorsements in AC 61-65K (issued 11/14/2025) — the FAA advisory circular that defines the sign-offs CFIs use to authorize student solos, cross-country flights, endorsements for higher-performance or tailwheel airplanes, flight reviews, and every category of checkride. Use the search bar above to find any endorsement by ID (e.g. A.9), FAR citation (e.g. 14 CFR 61.87), or alias (e.g. BFR, tailwheel, solo cross-country). Full list below so search engines and screen readers can read everything without JavaScript.

Training requirement snapshots by endorsement category

These short summaries connect the endorsement groups to the governing Part 61 or TSA rule text. They are a planning reference, not a substitute for checking the current eCFR, ACS, aircraft category/class, and local DPE expectations.

Practical-test readiness requirements

These endorsements are not a training stage by themselves; they document that the applicant has met the applicable certificate/rating requirements and is ready for the practical test.

  • Recent preparation: The practical-test recommendation requires logged preparation within the required recent window before the application.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.39(a)(6)(i) and (ii); AC 61-65K A.1

  • AKTR review: If the applicant has knowledge-test deficiencies, the instructor must review those areas and endorse satisfactory knowledge.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.39(a)(6)(iii); AC 61-65K A.2

Source review: 2026-07-06

Student pilot and solo requirements

Student-pilot solo privileges are built from eligibility, documents, medical status when required, TSA verification when applicable, and the specific solo endorsements for the operation.

  • Student pilot eligibility: Confirm student pilot eligibility and certificate application before solo; dual training may begin before every solo prerequisite is complete.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.83; 14 CFR § 61.85

  • Medical or qualifying status: Most airplane students on the private/recreational path need the required medical qualification before solo.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.3(c); 14 CFR § 61.23

  • Solo training and authorization: Pre-solo knowledge, flight training, and 90-day solo authority come from the applicable paragraphs of 14 CFR § 61.87.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.87(b); 14 CFR § 61.87(c); 14 CFR § 61.87(n); 14 CFR § 61.87(p)

  • Expanded student operations: Nearby-airport, solo cross-country, and Class B operations require separate training and endorsement paths.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.93; 14 CFR § 61.95

  • TSA verification: Flight training providers must handle citizenship or eligibility verification when applicable.

    References: 49 CFR § 1552.15(c); AC 61-65K A.14

Source review: 2026-07-06

Sport pilot requirement snapshot

Sport pilot requirements depend on category/class and privilege sought. For ASEL, the app tracks the common minimum-hour structure already used by the calculator.

  • Knowledge and proficiency: Sport applicants need the applicable knowledge, proficiency, and practical-test preparation for the aircraft category/class sought.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.309; 14 CFR § 61.311

  • ASEL hour minimums: For sport pilot ASEL, plan around 20 hours total, including 15 hours dual ASEL and 5 hours solo ASEL, plus required cross-country, landing, and recent-prep events.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.313

  • Added privileges: Towered airspace, faster light-sport aircraft, night, retractable gear, and controllable-pitch propeller privileges have separate rule paths.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.325; 14 CFR § 61.327; 14 CFR § 61.329; 14 CFR § 61.331

Source review: 2026-07-06

Recreational pilot requirement snapshot

Recreational pilot endorsements mostly support operating-limit relief, practical-test readiness, and supervised solo for additional ratings.

  • Eligibility and knowledge: Recreational applicants need the applicable eligibility, knowledge, and aeronautical experience requirements before practical-test endorsement.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.96; 14 CFR § 61.97; 14 CFR § 61.98; 14 CFR § 61.99

  • Operating limitations: The common recreational-pilot endorsements here remove or adjust specific operating limits such as distance, airspace, and recent PIC conditions.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.101

Source review: 2026-07-06

Private pilot requirement snapshot

Private pilot endorsement bundles prove the applicant met the certificate eligibility, knowledge, flight proficiency, and aeronautical experience rules for the rating sought.

  • Eligibility and knowledge: Confirm age, language, knowledge areas, and test preparation for the applicable private pilot rating.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.103; 14 CFR § 61.105

  • Flight proficiency: The applicant must receive and log training on the applicable areas of operation for the category/class rating sought.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.107

  • ASEL experience minimums: For private pilot ASEL, plan around 40 hours total, including 20 hours dual single-engine airplane and 10 hours solo single-engine airplane, plus the required XC, night, instrument, recent-prep, and towered-airport events.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.109(a)

Source review: 2026-07-06

Commercial pilot requirement snapshot

Commercial pilot endorsements document that the applicant meets the commercial eligibility, knowledge, proficiency, and aeronautical-experience rules for the rating sought.

  • Eligibility and knowledge: Confirm age, language, knowledge areas, and applicable commercial knowledge-test readiness.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.123; 14 CFR § 61.125

  • Flight proficiency: Commercial applicants must receive and log training on the applicable areas of operation for the aircraft category/class.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.127

  • ASEL experience minimums: For commercial pilot ASEL, plan around 250 hours total, 100 powered, 50 airplane, 100 PIC, 50 PIC XC, 20 commercial training, and 10 solo/PDPIC ASEL, plus the required instrument, complex/TAA/turbine, XC, night, and recent-prep events.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.129(a)

Source review: 2026-07-06

ATP and restricted ATP snapshot

ATP endorsements in AC 61-65K support ATP CTP completion and restricted ATP paths; the underlying qualification analysis depends heavily on category, class, age, total time, and training history.

  • ATP eligibility: Review the ATP eligibility, knowledge, and aeronautical experience rules before treating ATP endorsement text as sufficient.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.153; 14 CFR § 61.159

  • ATP CTP: ATP CTP completion is a prerequisite for certain ATP airplane multiengine knowledge-test paths.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.156

  • Restricted ATP: Restricted ATP eligibility depends on the qualifying pathway and must be checked against the specific paragraph.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.160

Source review: 2026-07-06

Instrument rating requirement snapshot

Instrument rating endorsements pair the knowledge-test path with the practical-test readiness path and the aeronautical experience required by 14 CFR § 61.65.

  • Eligibility and knowledge: Confirm the instrument rating eligibility, knowledge areas, and knowledge-test readiness before the checkride bundle.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.65(a); 14 CFR § 61.65(b)

  • Airplane experience minimums: For instrument airplane, plan around 50 hours XC PIC, including 10 in airplanes, and 40 hours actual/simulated instrument, including 15 hours with an authorized instructor for the instrument-airplane rating.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.65(d)

  • IFR XC and recent prep: The airplane path includes recent instrument training and the required IFR cross-country with approaches and navigation-system tasks.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.65(d)(2)(i); 14 CFR § 61.65(d)(2)(ii)

Source review: 2026-07-06

Flight instructor requirement snapshot

Flight instructor endorsements support FOI, instructor knowledge, practical-test readiness, spin training, and category/class-specific instructor privileges.

  • Eligibility and knowledge: Confirm flight instructor eligibility, fundamentals of instruction, aeronautical knowledge, and applicable knowledge-test paths.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.183; 14 CFR § 61.185

  • Instructor proficiency: Instructor applicants must receive and log training on the applicable instructor areas of operation and demonstrate instructional proficiency.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.187

  • Instructor limitations: Review CFI limitations before training initial instructor applicants or endorsing specialized operations.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.195

Source review: 2026-07-06

Sport pilot instructor requirement snapshot

Sport pilot instructor endorsements cover FOI, aeronautical knowledge, proficiency checks, practical-test readiness, and privilege-specific instructor training.

  • Tests and knowledge: Confirm the applicable sport pilot instructor tests, knowledge areas, and endorsement path.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.405; 14 CFR § 61.407

  • Proficiency and experience: Sport pilot instructor applicants must meet the applicable proficiency and aeronautical-experience rules for the privileges sought.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.409; 14 CFR § 61.411; 14 CFR § 61.419

  • Instrument-reference training: Certain sport pilot instructor privileges involving instrument-reference instruction require the specific training rule.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.412

Source review: 2026-07-06

Additional, recurrent, and add-on requirement snapshot

This category mixes recurrent checks, aircraft endorsements, additional ratings, retests, glider/tow operations, and other special cases. Read the specific endorsement card and the underlying rule together.

  • Recurrent proficiency: Flight reviews and instrument proficiency checks are recurrent-proficiency events, not certificate checkrides.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.56; 14 CFR § 61.57(d)

  • Aircraft endorsements: Complex, high-performance, high-altitude, tailwheel, and solo-without-category/class privileges each have their own aircraft-specific rule path.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.31

  • Add-ons and retests: Additional ratings and retests use separate rules from initial certificate hour minimums.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.63; 14 CFR § 61.49

  • Glider/tow operations: Tow and glider operations require the applicable training, experience, and endorsement checks.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.69

Source review: 2026-07-06

Robinson SFAR 73 snapshot

Robinson R-22/R-44 endorsements come from SFAR 73 and are additional to the normal Part 61 certificate, rating, flight review, and training rules.

  • Applicability: Confirm whether the operation involves R-22 or R-44 manipulation, PIC, instruction, or flight review duties covered by SFAR 73.

    References: 14 CFR Part 61 SFAR 73; AC 61-65K A.84-A.92

  • Awareness, experience, and proficiency: SFAR 73 separates awareness training, aeronautical experience, flight training, and flight-review/proficiency requirements.

    References: 14 CFR Part 61 SFAR 73 section 2

Source review: 2026-07-06

Specialty operations snapshot

NVG, EFVS, and simplified flight controls endorsements support narrow privileges. They should be paired with the aircraft, operation, and instructor-qualification rule text.

  • NVG and EFVS: Night vision goggle and enhanced flight vision system endorsements use specialized operating and training rules.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.31(k); 14 CFR § 61.31(l); 14 CFR § 61.45(h)(1)

  • Powered-lift instrument and simplified controls: Specialty entries in this category include powered-lift instrument and simplified-flight-controls instructor limitations.

    References: 14 CFR § 61.66; 14 CFR § 61.195(n)

Source review: 2026-07-06

14 CFR regulatory definitions and instructor decisions

The eCFR is authoritative but unofficial. This reference is a reviewed snapshot and is not represented as current after July 10, 2026. Reviewed 2026-07-13; snapshot through 2026-07-10.

§ 61.1(a) applicability

(a) Except as provided in parts 107 and 194 of this chapter, this part prescribes:
(1) The requirements for issuing pilot, flight instructor, and ground instructor certificates and ratings; the conditions under which those certificates and ratings are necessary; and the privileges and limitations of those certificates and ratings.
(2) The requirements for issuing pilot, flight instructor, and ground instructor authorizations; the conditions under which those authorizations are necessary; and the privileges and limitations of those authorizations.
(3) The requirements for issuing pilot, flight instructor, and ground instructor certificates and ratings for persons who have taken courses approved by the Administrator under other parts of this chapter.

Accredited

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Accredited has the same meaning as defined by the Department of Education in 34 CFR 600.2.

Aeronautical experience

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Aeronautical experience means pilot time obtained in an aircraft, flight simulator, or flight training device for meeting the appropriate training and flight time requirements for an airman certificate, rating, flight review, or recency of flight experience requirements of this part.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: A purpose-specific subset of pilot time used to satisfy Part 61 experience, review, or recency requirements.

Also searched as: experience requirements, aeronautical requirements

Authorized instructor

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Authorized instructor means—
(i) A person who holds a ground instructor certificate issued under part 61 of this chapter and is in compliance with § 61.217, when conducting ground training in accordance with the privileges and limitations of his or her ground instructor certificate;
(ii) A person who holds a flight instructor certificate issued under part 61 of this chapter and is in compliance with § 61.197, when conducting ground training or flight training in accordance with the privileges and limitations of his or her flight instructor certificate; or
(iii) A person authorized by the Administrator to provide ground training or flight training under part 61, 121, 135, or 142 of this chapter when conducting ground training or flight training in accordance with that authority.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: The authorization depends on the certificate or Administrator authority, current compliance, and the scope of the privileges being exercised.

Also searched as: who can instruct, authorized CFI, ground instructor

Aviation training device

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Aviation training device means a training device, other than a full flight simulator or flight training device, that has been evaluated, qualified, and approved by the Administrator.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: An FAA-evaluated training device that is not itself a full flight simulator or flight training device.

Also searched as: ATD, AATD, BATD

Complex airplane

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Complex airplane means an airplane that has a retractable landing gear, flaps, and a controllable pitch propeller, including airplanes equipped with an engine control system consisting of a digital computer and associated accessories for controlling the engine and propeller, such as a full authority digital engine control; or, in the case of a seaplane, flaps and a controllable pitch propeller, including seaplanes equipped with an engine control system consisting of a digital computer and associated accessories for controlling the engine and propeller, such as a full authority digital engine control.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: Complex status is equipment-defined. A technically advanced airplane is a separate concept and is not automatically complex.

Also searched as: complex aircraft, retractable gear airplane, complex endorsement

Cross-country time

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Cross-country time means—
(i) Except as provided in paragraphs (ii) through (vii) of this definition, time acquired during flight—
(A) Conducted by a person who holds a pilot certificate;
(B) Conducted in an aircraft;
(C) That includes a landing at a point other than the point of departure; and
(D) That involves the use of dead reckoning, pilotage, electronic navigation aids, radio aids, or other navigation systems to navigate to the landing point.
(ii) For the purpose of meeting the aeronautical experience requirements (except for a rotorcraft category rating), for a private pilot certificate (except for a powered parachute category rating), a commercial pilot certificate, or an instrument rating, or for the purpose of exercising recreational pilot privileges (except in a rotorcraft) under § 61.101 (c), time acquired during a flight—
(A) Conducted in an appropriate aircraft;
(B) That includes a point of landing that was at least a straight-line distance of more than 50 nautical miles from the original point of departure; and
(C) That involves the use of dead reckoning, pilotage, electronic navigation aids, radio aids, or other navigation systems to navigate to the landing point.
(iii) For the purpose of meeting the aeronautical experience requirements for a sport pilot certificate (except for powered parachute privileges), time acquired during a flight conducted in an appropriate aircraft that—
(A) Includes a point of landing at least a straight line distance of more than 25 nautical miles from the original point of departure; and
(B) Involves, as applicable, the use of dead reckoning; pilotage; electronic navigation aids; radio aids; or other navigation systems to navigate to the landing point.
(iv) For the purpose of meeting the aeronautical experience requirements for a sport pilot certificate with powered parachute privileges or a private pilot certificate with a powered parachute category rating, time acquired during a flight conducted in an appropriate aircraft that—
(A) Includes a point of landing at least a straight line distance of more than 15 nautical miles from the original point of departure; and
(B) Involves, as applicable, the use of dead reckoning; pilotage; electronic navigation aids; radio aids; or other navigation systems to navigate to the landing point.
(v) For the purpose of meeting the aeronautical experience requirements for any pilot certificate with a rotorcraft category rating or an instrument-helicopter rating, or for the purpose of exercising recreational pilot privileges, in a rotorcraft, under § 61.101(c), time acquired during a flight—
(A) Conducted in an appropriate aircraft;
(B) That includes a point of landing that was at least a straight-line distance of more than 25 nautical miles from the original point of departure; and
(C) That involves the use of dead reckoning, pilotage, electronic navigation aids, radio aids, or other navigation systems to navigate to the landing point.
(vi) For the purpose of meeting the aeronautical experience requirements for an airline transport pilot certificate (except with a rotorcraft category rating), time acquired during a flight—
(A) Conducted in an appropriate aircraft;
(B) That is at least a straight-line distance of more than 50 nautical miles from the original point of departure; and
(C) That involves the use of dead reckoning, pilotage, electronic navigation aids, radio aids, or other navigation systems.
(vii) For a military pilot who qualifies for a commercial pilot certificate (except with a rotorcraft category rating) under § 61.73 of this part, time acquired during a flight—
(A) Conducted in an appropriate aircraft;
(B) That is at least a straight-line distance of more than 50 nautical miles from the original point of departure; and
(C) That involves the use of dead reckoning, pilotage, electronic navigation aids, radio aids, or other navigation systems.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: The applicable branch depends on why the time is being counted and on certificate/category context; one distance rule does not govern every use.

Also searched as: XC, XC time, cross country, 50 NM, 25 NM, 15 NM

Examiner

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Examiner means any person who is authorized by the Administrator to conduct a pilot proficiency test or a practical test for an airman certificate or rating issued under this part, or a person who is authorized to conduct a knowledge test under this part.

Flight training

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Flight training means that training, other than ground training, received from an authorized instructor in flight in an aircraft.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: Part 61 flight training is received from an authorized instructor in flight in an aircraft.

Also searched as: dual received, flight instruction

Ground training

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Ground training means that training, other than flight training, received from an authorized instructor.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: Ground training is instruction received from an authorized instructor that is not flight training.

Also searched as: ground instruction, ground school

Institution of higher education

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Institution of higher education has the same meaning as defined by the Department of Education in 34 CFR 600.4.

Instrument approach

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Instrument approach means an approach procedure defined in part 97 of this chapter.

Instrument training

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Instrument training means that time in which instrument training is received from an authorized instructor under actual or simulated instrument conditions.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: Instrument training requires receipt of training from an authorized instructor under actual or simulated instrument conditions.

Also searched as: instrument instruction, CFII time

Knowledge test

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Knowledge test means a test on the aeronautical knowledge areas required for an airman certificate or rating that can be administered in written form or by a computer.

Nationally recognized accrediting agency

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Nationally recognized accrediting agency has the same meaning as defined by the Department of Education in 34 CFR 600.2.

Night vision goggles

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Night vision goggles means an appliance worn by a pilot that enhances the pilot's ability to maintain visual surface reference at night.

Night vision goggle operation

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Night vision goggle operation means the portion of a flight that occurs during the time period from 1 hour after sunset to 1 hour before sunrise where the pilot maintains visual surface reference using night vision goggles in an aircraft that is approved for such an operation.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: The NVG-operation window in §61.1 is not the same period as the general §1.1 definition of night.

Also searched as: NVG operation, NVGO

Passenger

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Passenger means any person on board an aircraft other than a crewmember, FAA personnel, manufacturer personnel required for type certification, or a person receiving or providing flight training, checking, or testing as authorized by this part.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: The definition excludes specified occupants, including a person receiving or providing authorized training, checking, or testing.

Also searched as: who is a passenger

Pilot time

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Pilot time means that time in which a person—
(i) Serves as a required pilot flight crewmember;
(ii) Receives training from an authorized instructor in an aircraft, full flight simulator, flight training device, or aviation training device;
(iii) Gives training as an authorized instructor in an aircraft, full flight simulator, flight training device, or aviation training device; or
(iv) Serves as second in command in operations conducted in accordance with § 135.99(c) of this chapter when a second pilot is not required under the type certification of the aircraft or the regulations under which the flight is being conducted, provided the requirements in § 61.159(c) are satisfied.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: Pilot time is the umbrella defined in §61.1; a specific logging rule still controls whether and how an entry may be recorded.

Also searched as: loggable pilot time, pilot hours

Practical test

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Practical test means a test on the areas of operations for an airman certificate, rating, or authorization that is conducted by having the applicant respond to questions and demonstrate maneuvers in flight, in a flight simulator, or in a flight training device.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: The regulatory definition includes questions and demonstration of maneuvers in an authorized test environment.

Also searched as: checkride, oral and flight test

Set of aircraft

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Set of aircraft means aircraft that share similar performance characteristics, such as similar airspeed and altitude operating envelopes, similar handling characteristics, and the same number and type of propulsion systems.

Student pilot seeking a sport pilot certificate

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Student pilot seeking a sport pilot certificate means a person who has received an endorsement—
(i) To exercise student pilot privileges from a certificated flight instructor with a sport pilot rating; or
(ii) That includes a limitation for the operation of an aircraft specified in § 61.89(c) issued by a certificated flight instructor with other than a sport pilot rating.

Technically advanced airplane (TAA)

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Technically advanced airplane (TAA) means an airplane equipped with an electronically advanced avionics system.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: TAA is defined by electronically advanced avionics and is distinct from the equipment test for a complex airplane.

Also searched as: TAA, technically advanced aircraft, glass cockpit airplane, commercial TAA

Training time

Source: 14 CFR § 61.1 · dated snapshot

Training time means training received—
(i) In flight from an authorized instructor;
(ii) On the ground from an authorized instructor; or
(iii) In a flight simulator or flight training device from an authorized instructor.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: Training time includes authorized instruction in flight, on the ground, or in a qualifying simulator or flight training device.

Also searched as: instruction time, training hours

Aircraft

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Aircraft means a device that is used or intended to be used for flight in the air.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: The chapter-wide definition is intentionally broad and turns on use or intended use for flight in the air.

Also searched as: what is an aircraft

Category

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Category:
(1) As used with respect to the certification, ratings, privileges, and limitations of airmen, means a broad classification of aircraft. Examples include: airplane; rotorcraft; glider; and lighter-than-air; and
(2) As used with respect to the certification of aircraft, means a grouping of aircraft based upon intended use or operating limitations. Examples include: transport, normal, utility, acrobatic, limited, restricted, and provisional.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: For airman certification, category is the broad level such as airplane or rotorcraft; the same word has a different aircraft-certification meaning.

Also searched as: aircraft category, airman category, airplane rotorcraft glider

Class

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Class:
(1) As used with respect to the certification, ratings, privileges, and limitations of airmen, means a classification of aircraft within a category having similar operating characteristics. Examples include: single engine; multiengine; land; water; gyroplane; helicopter; airship; and free balloon; and
(2) As used with respect to the certification of aircraft, means a broad grouping of aircraft having similar characteristics of propulsion, flight, or landing. Examples include: airplane; rotorcraft; glider; balloon; landplane; and seaplane.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: For airman certification, class sits within a category, such as single-engine land within airplane.

Also searched as: aircraft class, airman class, single engine land, ASEL, AMEL

Crewmember

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Crewmember means a person assigned to perform duty in an aircraft during flight time.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: Crewmember status depends on assignment to duty in the aircraft during flight time.

Also searched as: crew member

Flight time

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Flight time means:
(1) Pilot time that commences when an aircraft moves under its own power for the purpose of flight and ends when the aircraft comes to rest after landing; or
(2) For a glider without self-launch capability, pilot time that commences when the glider is towed for the purpose of flight and ends when the glider comes to rest after landing.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: The chapter-wide start/stop definition is not simply a meter reading; the aircraft movement must be for the purpose of flight.

Also searched as: block time, aircraft time, hobbs time

Flight training device (FTD)

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Flight training device (FTD) means a replica of aircraft instruments, equipment, panels, and controls in an open flight deck area or an enclosed aircraft cockpit replica. It includes the equipment and computer programs necessary to represent aircraft (or set of aircraft) operations in ground and flight conditions having the full range of capabilities of the systems installed in the device as described in part 60 of this chapter and the qualification performance standard (QPS) for a specific FTD qualification level.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: An FTD is a qualified aircraft cockpit or flight-deck replica governed by Part 60 and its qualification standard.

Also searched as: FTD, flight training device, training device

Full flight simulator (FFS)

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Full flight simulator (FFS) means a replica of a specific type; or make, model, and series aircraft cockpit. It includes the assemblage of equipment and computer programs necessary to represent aircraft operations in ground and flight conditions, a visual system providing an out-of-the-cockpit view, a system that provides cues at least equivalent to those of a three-degree-of-freedom motion system, and has the full range of capabilities of the systems installed in the device as described in part 60 of this chapter and the qualification performance standards (QPS) for a specific FFS qualification level.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: A full flight simulator is the Part 60 device with a cockpit replica, outside visual system, and qualifying motion cues.

Also searched as: flight simulator, FFS, full flight simulator, simulator

Night

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Night means the time between the end of evening civil twilight and the beginning of morning civil twilight, as published in the Air Almanac, converted to local time.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: This definition governs the term night, but separate rules can use different sunset/sunrise windows for specific privileges or equipment.

Also searched as: FAA night, civil twilight, night time

Pilot in command

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Pilot in command means the person who:
(1) Has final authority and responsibility for the operation and safety of the flight;
(2) Has been designated as pilot in command before or during the flight; and
(3) Holds the appropriate category, class, and type rating, if appropriate, for the conduct of the flight.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: This §1.1 definition identifies the person acting as PIC. Logging PIC time is governed separately by §61.51.

Also searched as: PIC, acting PIC, pilot in command

Second in command

Source: 14 CFR § 1.1 · dated snapshot

Second in command means a pilot who is designated to be second in command of an aircraft during flight time.

Instructor planning explanation — not CFR text: Designation as SIC is distinct from whether SIC is required and whether the time may be logged under a specific rule.

Also searched as: SIC, copilot, second in command

Instructor decision guides

Flight time, pilot time, and training time

Start with the defined bucket, then apply the specific logging and credit rule for the purpose at hand.

Instructor planning conclusion — not CFR text.

  • Aircraft movement: for the purpose of flight — Use the §1.1 flight-time start/stop definition; a meter alone does not decide the entry.
  • Pilot role or instruction: serves / receives / gives — Test the §61.1 pilot-time branches, then the applicable §61.51 logging rule.
  • Training credit: received from an authorized instructor — Identify flight, ground, simulator, or FTD training and verify the rule permits that device and credit.

Review: 14 CFR §§ 1.1, 61.1, 61.51

Acting as PIC versus logging PIC

The person with final authority under §1.1 and the person permitted to log PIC under §61.51 are related questions, not interchangeable labels.

Instructor planning conclusion — not CFR text.

  • Who is acting as PIC?: final authority and responsibility / designated / holds the appropriate ratings — Apply the three-part §1.1 definition to identify the acting PIC.
  • Who may log PIC?: only for that flight time during which — Apply the applicable §61.51(e) path; do not infer logging authority solely from who acted as PIC.
  • Training flight: receives training / gives training — Student and instructor entries may arise from different logging provisions even though only one person acts as PIC.

Review: 14 CFR §§ 1.1, 61.1, 61.51(e)

Cross-country purpose, category, and distance

Choose the purpose branch before applying a distance threshold. The general landing-away definition and aeronautical-experience branches are not interchangeable.

Instructor planning conclusion — not CFR text.

  • General cross-country time: Except as provided in paragraphs (ii) through (vii) — Use §61.1(b) cross-country paragraph (i): certificate, aircraft, landing away, and navigation elements.
  • Private/commercial/instrument aeronautical experience, non-rotorcraft: includes a point of landing that was at least a straight-line distance of more than 50 nautical miles from the original point of departure — Use paragraph (ii), including an appropriate aircraft and a landing beyond the stated straight-line distance.
  • Sport pilot, except powered parachute: Includes a point of landing at least a straight line distance of more than 25 nautical miles from the original point of departure — Use paragraph (iii) and preserve the rule's full threshold wording when briefing or auditing.
  • Powered parachute path: Includes a point of landing at least a straight line distance of more than 15 nautical miles from the original point of departure — Use paragraph (iv) for the specified sport/private powered-parachute purposes.
  • Rotorcraft or instrument-helicopter path: includes a point of landing that was at least a straight-line distance of more than 25 nautical miles from the original point of departure — Use paragraph (v), including the appropriate-aircraft and landing requirements.
  • ATP or qualifying military path: is at least a straight-line distance of more than 50 nautical miles from the original point of departure — Use paragraph (vi) or (vii); these branches do not require a landing in their text.

Review: 14 CFR § 61.1(b), definition of Cross-country time

Instrument time versus instrument training

Instrument conditions describe the operation; instrument training additionally requires instruction from an authorized instructor.

Instructor planning conclusion — not CFR text.

  • Actual or simulated instrument conditions: under actual or simulated instrument conditions — Evaluate the applicable §61.51 instrument-time logging provision.
  • Instrument training credit: received from an authorized instructor — Verify instructor authority, training context, and the certificate/rating rule that permits the credit.

Review: 14 CFR §§ 61.1, 61.51(g), and the applicable aeronautical-experience section

Authorized-instructor scope

Holding an instructor credential is only one element; the person must be compliant and acting within that credential or other FAA authority.

Instructor planning conclusion — not CFR text.

  • Ground instructor: in compliance with § 61.217 / in accordance with the privileges and limitations — Confirm recent-experience compliance and the ground-instructor certificate's permitted scope.
  • Flight instructor: in compliance with § 61.197 / in accordance with the privileges and limitations — Confirm recent-experience status plus certificate category, class, instrument, and any rule-specific limitations.
  • Other Administrator authority: under part 61, 121, 135, or 142 / in accordance with that authority — Review the particular authorization; do not treat it as unrestricted Part 61 CFI authority.

Review: 14 CFR § 61.1(b), definition of Authorized instructor

Complex airplane versus TAA

These are separate equipment definitions. An airplane can satisfy one, both, or neither definition.

Instructor planning conclusion — not CFR text.

  • Complex airplane: retractable landing gear, flaps, and a controllable pitch propeller — Apply every listed equipment element and the seaplane alternative exactly as written. For acting as PIC, separately apply §61.31(e)'s training and one-time logbook-endorsement rule, including its exceptions.
  • Technically advanced airplane: equipped with an electronically advanced avionics system — For commercial airplane single-engine credit, separately confirm §61.129(a)(3)(ii) and §61.129(j): an installed electronic PFD, an electronic MFD with a GPS moving map and aircraft position, and a two-axis autopilot integrated with navigation and heading guidance. The required PFD and MFD display elements must be continuously visible.

Review: 14 CFR §§ 61.1(b), 61.31(e), 61.129(a)(3)(ii), and 61.129(j)

Frequently asked questions about FAA endorsements

What is AC 61-65?

FAA Advisory Circular 61-65 provides the model endorsement text that certificated flight instructors (CFIs) and ground instructors use to document training and authorize specific privileges in a pilot's logbook. The current revision is AC 61-65K (issued 11/14/2025).

Do CFIs have to copy the AC 61-65 endorsement text word-for-word?

The exact wording is not mandatory, but it is strongly recommended. Using the model text ensures all required elements are present and reduces the risk of a rejected endorsement at a checkride or FAA ramp check. Simply Endorsed CFI shows the verbatim text for every endorsement alongside a plain-language explanation.

What makes Simply Endorsed CFI useful as an endorsement lookup?

Simply Endorsed CFI is a free, search-first tool built specifically for AC 61-65K (the current 2025 revision). Every endorsement is searchable by ID, FAR citation, alias, or checkride use case, and every endorsement links to its source page in the FAA's PDF. There is no account, no paywall, and no ads.

Which endorsement do I need for a first solo?

First solo endorsements come from 14 CFR 61.87 and typically include A.3 (pre-solo aeronautical knowledge), A.4 (pre-solo flight training), and A.6 (solo flight in a specific make and model). Search "first solo" in the tool above for the complete list with verbatim text.
