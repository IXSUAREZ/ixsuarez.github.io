# Safety Model and Instructional Boundaries

This is an educational preflight hazard-identification aid. It is not a dispatch release, official weather briefing, regulatory determination, aircraft performance calculator, or guarantee that a flight is safe.

## Core decisions

- **PAVE is the organizing framework.** Pilot questions include IMSAFE; Aircraft, enVironment, and External pressures remain distinct so students can see where risk originates.
- **Risk is not an additive point total.** Every applicable factor is evaluated independently and the highest unresolved result controls the overall result.
- **The decision picture stays visible.** Users see Stop, Review, and Verify counts plus the highest concerns.
- **Incomplete means incomplete.** Missing critical answers prevent the tool from reporting a complete result.
- **Credentials are context, not credit.** Student, private, commercial, and ATP selections never grant a lower result or automatically increase personal minimums.
- **Personal limits are deliberate setup.** The FAA framework leaves numeric values to the pilot, based on recent experience, proficiency, aircraft, and operation. The tool stores blank-by-default values separately from each flight and never invents certificate-tier presets.
- **Student constraints are not recommendations.** The 3 SM day/5 SM night solo visibility limits in § 61.89, current endorsements, and instructor logbook limitations are legal constraints. A CFI may set more restrictive limits.
- **Context changes invalidate answers.** Switching role, rules, or day/night clears per-flight responses so a stale result cannot carry into a different plan.
- **Saved data fails closed.** Missing, legacy, or unrecognized answer values remain Incomplete and can never produce a Low result.
- **Mitigation changes the plan.** Delaying, changing runways, adding fuel, taking an instructor, choosing a different procedure, or cancelling can alter the relevant input. Simply acknowledging a hazard does not lower it.

## Result language

- **Low:** Continue full planning and monitor for changes. Low does not mean safe.
- **Moderate:** Mitigate where practical, discuss when appropriate, and reassess before departure.
- **Stop:** Do not depart on the current plan. Change or remove the condition, confirm the new facts, and reassess.
- **Incomplete:** Answer all applicable safety-critical questions before using the result.

The pilot in command remains responsible for complying with regulations, operating limitations, weather and NOTAM review, aircraft performance and airworthiness, and sound aeronautical decision-making.
