What's included

A real first lesson, not a sightseeing ride

A discovery flight is an introductory flight lesson: a short ground briefing, about an hour of airplane time, and a debrief afterward. You sit in the left seat — the pilot's seat — and once we're clear of the traffic pattern, the controls are yours.

It's also not a test. You don't need to perform, prepare, or know anything in advance. Most first-timers fly better than they expect, because the airplane is more stable than they expect.

Every flight includes

  • Pre-flight briefing — what will happen and how the controls work
  • Guided pre-flight inspection of the airplane
  • About an hour in the air over Louisville
  • Headset provided — no gear to buy or rent
  • Hands-on flying: straight-and-level, climbs, descents, turns
  • Debrief, a logbook entry, and an honest next-step conversation
What to expect

How the flight actually goes

Plan on about two hours total at Bowman Field. We start with a short intake and a briefing, walk around the airplane together, and then go fly. I handle the takeoff; once we're clear of the pattern, you fly — straight-and-level first, then climbs, descents, and turns over Louisville. Churchill Downs, the Ohio River, and downtown are typically visible from our normal practice area.

I take the controls back for the landing, and most first-timers tell me watching a real landing up close was their favorite part. Back in the office, we debrief and log the flight — those hours count toward any certificate you pursue later.

What to bring

Show up as you are

You don't need a headset (I provide one), a logbook (I'll start one for you if you want), or any prior knowledge. The whole packing list:

  • Sunglasses — non-polarized is slightly better for glass-panel displays
  • Comfortable shoes — you'll use the rudder pedals; no flip-flops
  • Layers — cockpit temperature varies with the season
  • A light breakfast — steady blood sugar, calm stomach
  • An empty bladder — there's no bathroom in the airplane
Weather & rescheduling

Bad weather? We reschedule. Simple.

I don't squeeze discovery flights into marginal weather. The point of the flight is a clean, honest first experience — not surviving turbulence. If the forecast doesn't cooperate, we pick a new time, and calm morning or evening air usually gives the smoothest first impression.

If you're prone to motion sickness, mention it when booking and we'll aim for the smoothest part of the day. It's almost never a problem on calm-morning flights.

Common questions

Discovery flight FAQs

How much is a discovery flight in Louisville?
Instructor time on a discovery flight is $45 per hour, plus the aircraft rental for the flight — I'll quote you the exact all-in total before you commit to anything. The quote covers the airplane, the instructor, the briefing, and the debrief — no hidden add-ons.
How long does it take?
About an hour in the airplane, plus 30–40 minutes of briefing and debrief on the ground. Budget about two hours total at Bowman Field.
Do I actually get to fly the plane?
Yes. "Hands-on controls" is not a marketing phrase. Once we're clear of the traffic pattern, you'll fly straight-and-level, climbs, descents, and turns — the same fundamental maneuvers every pilot learns on lesson one. I keep my hands close to my own controls the whole time (standard procedure), but the flying is yours. I handle the takeoff and landing.
What if weather cancels the flight?
We reschedule. I don't fly discovery flights in marginal weather — the point is a clean, enjoyable first experience, and calm air makes a much better first impression. If the forecast doesn't cooperate, we simply pick a new time.
Can I buy a discovery flight as a gift?
Absolutely — it's one of the most popular reasons people book. Contact me with the occasion and we'll arrange it: you book, they fly, and scheduling happens with whoever you prefer.
What happens after the flight?
Whatever you want. If you're hooked, we can plan next steps — medical certificate, ground study, a realistic training schedule — and your discovery flight hour already counts toward a future certificate. If you decide flying isn't for you, you got a great hour and an honest answer, and you saved yourself thousands by finding out early. Both outcomes are legitimate.
